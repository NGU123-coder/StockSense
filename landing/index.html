Two ways distributors lose money

Fast sellers run out

Orders go in by gut feel or a fixed monthly rhythm. A festive spike or a late supplier empties the shelf, and the customer buys elsewhere.

Slow stock eats cash

Meanwhile the warehouse holds four months of an item that sells six units a day. That money could have bought the products that are running out.

Nobody sees the whole picture

Stock lives in four warehouses and three spreadsheets. By the time the numbers are combined, the decision is already late.

How it works

  1. Forecast every product, every night

    A Python service fits a Holt-Winters model to each product’s daily sales in each warehouse, learning its weekly rhythm and trend, and checks its own accuracy on the last four weeks.

  2. Turn forecasts into a buying plan

    Lead time, demand variation and your target service level become a safety stock, a reorder point and an order quantity, with stock already on order taken into account.

  3. Order in one click, with approval

    The planner drafts purchase orders grouped by supplier. A manager approves them, the supplier gets a PDF, and receiving the goods updates stock everywhere in real time.

Built with

A production-style setup: two services, one database, real-time updates and tests at every layer.

Core API
Node.js, Express 5, Drizzle ORM, JWT with rotating refresh tokens, role-based access, Socket.io, BullMQ

Delivery
Docker Compose locally, Vercel and Render in production
